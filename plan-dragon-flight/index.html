Project: Isekai Gacha

Plan — the dragon’s flight

The working plan the flight was rebuilt from. Problem framing, the decisions and why, and the units the work was broken into.

Summary

The dragon currently flies well but performs rarely. It holds one perch per layer, moves three times across the whole page, travels in a straight line, and plays one of two wing cycles. Between layer changes it is pinned to the screen and does nothing.

This plan rebuilds the motion underneath it so that the flight is frame-rate independent, the routes are curved and choreographed, the dragon is always doing something, and it performs real manoeuvres — barrel rolls, loops, wingovers and diving flares — chosen by the shape of the route rather than by a timer. It also gives it a proper clip vocabulary: powered flight, glide, hover, take-off, landing and a grounded idle, blended in phase so the wings never jump mid-beat.

Everything stays inside the creature layer. No copy, layout, colour, roadmap or design-system code is touched.

Problem Frame

Reading the current implementation from the visitor's seat surfaces four concrete defects, not just an absence of polish.

The scroll wheel drives the physics. The spring target is camera.position + offset in world space, and the camera travels twenty-six units down the scene. Scrolling therefore drags the target through the world and the spring chases it, so s.velocity is dominated by camera motion rather than by the dragon's own intent. Fast scrolling pushes speed past FLYING, which flips it to the travel clip and pitches it from -velocity.y, while on screen it has not moved at all. The banking a visitor sees is mostly a readout of how fast they are scrolling.

Yaw takes the long way round. g.rotation.y is lerped toward an atan2 result, which lives in (-π, π]. Any transit that crosses that boundary spins the dragon the wrong way through most of a full turn.

Smoothing is tied to refresh rate. Every easing in the file is Math.min(1, delta * rate) — a linear approximation of exponential decay — and the spring is integrated with a raw variable Euler step. Both mean the motion is measurably different at 60Hz, 90Hz and 120Hz, and delta clamped at 1/30 makes anything slower than thirty frames a second run in slow motion.

There is almost nothing to watch. Four perches produce three transits. Each is a straight line. Between them the dragon holds a fixed screen position with a single looping hover.

Separately, the rig carries thirty-one clips and the site uses four. Glide, TakeOff, Falling and GlideToLanding are all present, converted by the same pipeline, and unused.

High-Level Technical Design

The per-frame pipeline

Today the frame body does everything inline in one function. The redesign runs the same data through named stages, most of which become pure functions in src/webgl/flight.ts and are unit-testable without a WebGL context.

flowchart TD
    S[scroll progress] --> ST[station: which resting place, from progress]
    ST -->|station changed| RT[route: build a cubic Bézier<br/>out along current velocity,<br/>in along arrival facing]
    ST -->|holding| DR[drift: slow deterministic bob<br/>around the station]
    RT --> LP[lead point: advance u along the curve<br/>at a rate tied to own speed]
    DR --> AIM
    LP --> AIM[aim point, in camera-local space]
    AIM --> SP[spring + drag, fixed 1/120 substeps]
    SP --> V[local velocity]
    V --> AT[attitude: bank from vx, pitch from vy,<br/>heading from atan2, shortest arc]
    RT --> MV[manoeuvre: chosen from route geometry]
    MV --> AT
    V --> CL[clip state machine + effort]
    AT --> OUT[world transform = camera position + local]
    CL --> OUT

The single most important change is that the spring solves in camera-local space. s.position and s.velocity become offsets from the camera; the world transform is composed at the end. Camera motion then cannot inject velocity, and every downstream reading of velocity — banking, clip choice, wingbeat rate, manoeuvre eligibility — describes what the dragon is actually doing.

Scroll can then be reintroduced as a chosen input rather than a leak: a small slipstream term that nudges the local aim point back and up while the reader is moving fast, and recovers when they stop. Directional guidance, magnitude to be found during implementation.

Clip state machine

Six clips, with hysteresis on every speed threshold so nothing chatters at the boundary. Today a single speed > 0.9 test flips fly/hover on and off when the dragon idles near that speed.

stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> hover
    hover --> fly: speed > enter
    fly --> hover: speed < leave
    fly --> glide: descending and coasting
    glide --> fly: climbing, or accelerating
    glide --> hover: speed < leave
    hover --> landing: at the bottom, settling
    fly --> landing: at the bottom, settling
    glide --> landing: at the bottom, settling
    landing --> ground: settled
    ground --> takeoff: reader scrolls back up
    takeoff --> fly: launch impulse spent

Transitions cross-fade with a per-pair duration rather than one constant, and the wing cycles are phase matched — the incoming action's time is set to the same proportional point in its own cycle that the outgoing action is at, so the wings continue their beat through the blend instead of jumping. fly, hover and glide are all wing cycles of different lengths; this is the last remaining source of the "clippy" look.

Choosing a manoeuvre

Manoeuvres are picked from the geometry of the route that was just built, so they read as motivated rather than random. Nothing fires on a short hop, and nothing fires whose swept volume would reach the reading column.

flowchart TD
    N[new route built] --> L{length}
    L -->|short| NONE[no manoeuvre]
    L -->|medium or long| D{dominant axis}
    D -->|descending| DIVE[dive and flare:<br/>glide, spring stiffened,<br/>hard flap on arrival]
    D -->|climbing| WO[wingover:<br/>roll in, pull up,<br/>roll out the other way]
    D -->|lateral| BR[barrel roll: roll += 2π]
    L -->|very long| LOOP[loop: pitch += 2π<br/>with a vertical bulge in the path]
    DIVE --> C{swept volume clears<br/>the reading column?}
    WO --> C
    BR --> C
    LOOP --> C
    C -->|yes| GO[perform]
    C -->|no| DOWN[downgrade to the next tamer option,<br/>or skip]

A manoeuvre is an additive attitude channel — roll, pitch and yaw offsets summed on top of the physics-derived attitude and eased with smootherstep — plus, for the loop and the dive, a modification to the route's control points. It never replaces the physics, so acceleration and settle still fall out of the spring.

Selection must be deterministic (C5). Derive it from the station index and the route's own geometry, or from a seeded sequence in the style of the mote scatter in src/webgl/Atmosphere.tsx.

Key Technical Decisions

Solve the spring in camera-local space rather than world space. This is the root fix for scroll contamination, and it makes every velocity-derived readout downstream mean what its name says. The alternative — subtracting camera velocity from s.velocity before reading it — treats the symptom and leaves the integration itself distorted.

Integrate the spring at a fixed timestep with an accumulator. A spring integrated with variable Euler steps has framerate-dependent damping, which is precisely the "not quite buttery" the request is aimed at. Substepping at 1/120 makes the trajectory identical on every device. Cost is bounded — at most a handful of substeps per frame once delta is clamped.

Replace linear smoothing with exponential decay. 1 - Math.exp(-rate * delta) instead of Math.min(1, delta * rate), everywhere, including the foot-lift correction. Same tuning intent, correct at any refresh rate.

Shape the route with a lead point on a cubic Bézier, not with keyframes. The spring keeps chasing a moving aim; only the aim's path changes. Acceleration, overshoot and settle still emerge from the physics, and the existing principle that none of the flight is keyframed survives intact. Control points pulled out along the departure velocity and in along the arrival facing give tangent continuity at both ends — that is what makes a route read as choreographed rather than interpolated.

Extract the motion maths into src/webgl/flight.ts as pure functions. The existing dragon tests assert against regular expressions matched over the source file, and HANDOFF.md already records that several broke on refactors that preserved behaviour. Pure functions taking numbers and vectors and returning numbers and vectors can be tested for what they actually do, with no WebGL context. This is the change that makes the rest of the plan safely testable.

Manoeuvres are additive and gated by a clearance envelope. They ride on top of the physics rather than replacing it, and any manoeuvre whose swept volume reaches the reading column is downgraded or skipped. C3 is the constraint most at risk from this plan and it needs a structural guard, not a tuned constant.

Add both Glide and TakeOff. Confirmed with the user. Glide is what makes a descent read as a descent rather than a slow fall; take-off is what makes scrolling back up feel like a release rather than a drift.

Implementation Units

U1. Frame-rate-independent flight core, solved in camera-local space

Goal: the existing behaviour, but correct — identical at any refresh rate, free of scroll contamination, and with the motion maths extracted where it can be tested directly.

Requirements: R1. Preserves C1, C6.

Dependencies: none. Everything else builds on this.

Files:

  • create src/webgl/flight.ts — shortestAngle, approach (exponential decay), stepSpring (fixed-timestep accumulator), bankFrom, pitchFrom, headingFrom
  • create src/webgl/flight.test.ts
  • modify src/webgl/Creatures.tsx
  • modify src/webgl/atmosphere.test.tsx — see "Tests that change" below

Approach: hold s.position and s.velocity as camera-relative offsets; integrate against the station offset directly; compose g.position = camera.position + s.position at the end of the frame. Replace every Math.min(1, delta * rate) with approach(rate, delta). Route all three rotation channels through shortestAngle before easing. Raise the delta clamp to 1/15 — still enough to catch the whole-second delta a backgrounded tab reports, without dilating time on a 25fps device — and let the fixed-timestep accumulator absorb the variation.

Patterns to follow: the preallocated scratch vectors in state.current — new scratch goes there, nothing is allocated in the frame body. src/scroll/motion.ts remains the only source of durations and limits for anything DOM-side; flight.ts owns the 3D constants.

Test scenarios (src/webgl/flight.test.ts unless noted):

  • shortestAngle from 3.0 to -3.0 returns roughly +0.28, not -6.0
  • shortestAngle is stable at exactly ±π and returns 0 for equal inputs
  • approach(rate, delta) returns a value in [0, 1] and approaches 1 as delta grows
  • Integrating a spring for one second at delta = 1/30 and at delta = 1/144 lands within a small tolerance of the same position — the framerate-independence claim, asserted rather than described
  • A spring released from a displacement with the settle damping ratio never overshoots past its target; with the patrol ratio it does
  • stepSpring given a delta of two seconds performs a bounded number of substeps rather than looping thousands of times
  • bankFrom clamps at the stated limits and is antisymmetric about zero
  • (atmosphere.test.tsx) The dragon's local velocity is unaffected by camera motion: driving the descent subscriber through a full page of progress with the station unchanged leaves the aim point fixed in camera-local space

Verification: scrolling quickly through a single layer no longer pitches or banks the dragon, and no longer flips it to the travel clip. Motion looks the same on a 60Hz and a 120Hz display.

U2. A flight plan — stations, and motion inside a layer

Goal: replace one-perch-per-layer with a route of stations advanced by scroll progress, and give it a slow drift while it holds, so the dragon is never inert.

Requirements: R5. Preserves C1, C3, C5.

Dependencies: U1.

Files:

  • create src/webgl/route.ts — the station table, stationAt(progress), and the drift function
  • create src/webgl/route.test.ts
  • modify src/webgl/Creatures.tsx
  • modify src/webgl/atmosphere.test.tsx

Approach: each station carries an offset, a resting attitude, and the band of scroll progress it owns. Selection is by progress rather than by layer index, so two or three stations can sit inside one layer. Bands are contiguous and cover [0, 1) with no gap, which is what stops the dragon sitting on the previous layer's paragraph while the next one scrolls past — the reason the current code keys on layer at all.

Drift is a slow, small-amplitude Lissajous applied to the station offset, deterministic from elapsed time and station index. Amplitude must stay inside the clearance margin so drift can never push a wingtip over the text.

Patterns to follow: the deterministic scatter in src/webgl/Atmosphere.tsx (a fixed LCG, never Math.random). Station clearance mirrors the existing perch rule: |x| > 10, |y| > 6, |z| > 19.

Test scenarios (src/webgl/route.test.ts unless noted):

  • Bands are contiguous, ordered, and cover the full range — no progress value falls between two stations
  • stationAt(0) is the first station and stationAt(0.999) is the last
  • stationAt is monotonic: walking progress forward never returns to an earlier station
  • Every station clears the reading column on all three axes, drift amplitude included — the clearance test is applied to offset ± driftAmplitude, not to the bare offset
  • Consecutive stations alternate sides, so it never settles over the same column twice
  • Every station holds a nose-up, banked, three-quarter attitude (the existing perch-pose invariants, applied to the new table)
  • Drift is deterministic: the same elapsed time and station index return the same offset across calls
  • Drift returns zero displacement under reduced motion
  • There are meaningfully more stations than layers — asserted as a floor, so a future edit cannot quietly collapse it back to four

Verification: scrolling steadily through any single layer shows the dragon repositioning at least once, and it visibly breathes rather than sitting frozen while holding.

U3. Curved routes

Goal: transits arc rather than running straight, leaving along the direction the dragon was already travelling and arriving into the station's facing.

Requirements: R4, R2.

Dependencies: U1, U2.

Files:

  • modify src/webgl/flight.ts — cubic Bézier evaluation, control-point construction, arc-length-ish parameter advance
  • modify src/webgl/flight.test.ts
  • modify src/webgl/Creatures.tsx

Approach: on a station change, build a cubic from the current local position to the new station offset. The first control point is pulled along current velocity, the second back along the arrival facing; both are biased outward, away from the reading column, with the bias scaled by route length. A lead parameter u advances at a rate proportional to the dragon's own speed and to the inverse of the local curve derivative, so it does not sprint through the tight part of the arc. The spring chases the point at u; when u reaches 1 the aim becomes the station itself and drift resumes.

Technical design — directional, not specification:

control1 = from + normalize(velocity) * lead * length
control2 = to   - facingOf(station)    * lead * length
both nudged along outwardSign(from, to) so the belly of the arc
faces the screen edge rather than the text

Patterns to follow: the existing comment discipline in Creatures.tsx — every constant explains what it buys and what the alternative looked like.

Test scenarios:

  • A curve evaluated at u = 0 returns the start and at u = 1 returns the end
  • The curve's midpoint lies further from the reading column than the straight line between its endpoints — the arc bulges outward, not inward
  • No point sampled along the curve at any u violates the clearance rule
  • The departure tangent points along the supplied velocity, so a dragon already moving does not kink at the start of a new route
  • The arrival tangent points along the station facing
  • The lead parameter is monotonic in time and reaches exactly 1, never overshoots
  • A zero-length route (station unchanged) degenerates safely rather than dividing by zero
  • Under reduced motion the route is skipped entirely and the station is adopted directly

Verification: a transit from one side of the screen to the other traces a visible arc with its belly toward the screen edge, and the dragon banks into it because the curvature is now in the velocity.

U4. The manoeuvre layer

Goal: loops, barrel rolls, wingovers and diving flares, chosen by the shape of the route, gated so they can never reach the text.

Requirements: R3, R6. Preserves C1, C3, C5, C7.

Dependencies: U3.

Files:

  • create src/webgl/manoeuvres.ts — the catalogue, the selector, the clearance envelope, and the additive attitude channel
  • create src/webgl/manoeuvres.test.ts
  • modify src/webgl/Creatures.tsx
  • modify src/webgl/atmosphere.test.tsx

Approach: a manoeuvre is a named entry with a duration, an additive attitude curve over normalised time, and an optional control-point modifier for the route. Rotation offsets are summed onto the physics attitude after shortestAngle resolution — since a barrel roll deliberately runs past π, the additive channel is accumulated in its own unwrapped space and added, never fed back through the shortest-arc path.

Selection reads the route: length, dominant axis, and sign. Descending and long becomes a dive and flare; climbing and long a wingover; lateral a barrel roll; very long a loop. Short hops get nothing. The chosen manoeuvre's swept volume is tested against the clearance rule before it is committed; failing that it downgrades — loop to wingover, wingover to barrel roll, barrel roll to nothing.

The dive and flare is the one manoeuvre that touches the physics: it stiffens the spring and drops the drag for its duration, then restores them. This is where the acceleration in R2 becomes unmistakable.

Patterns to follow: the existing SETTLE_SPRING / SETTLE_DRAG pair, which already demonstrates swapping the physics constants for a phase of flight.

Test scenarios (src/webgl/manoeuvres.test.ts unless noted):

  • A barrel roll's additive roll starts and ends at zero modulo 2π, and passes through π at the midpoint — it completes a full revolution rather than rocking
  • A loop's additive pitch completes a full revolution over its duration
  • Both eased curves have zero derivative at both ends, so the manoeuvre neither starts nor stops with a jerk
  • Selection is a pure function of route geometry: identical routes select identical manoeuvres across calls (C5)
  • A short route selects nothing
  • A descending route selects the dive; a climbing route the wingover; a lateral route the barrel roll
  • A manoeuvre whose swept volume crosses the reading column is downgraded, and the downgrade chain terminates at "nothing" rather than recursing
  • A loop's route bulge is directed away from the reading column, never through it
  • The dive restores the original spring and drag when it ends, including when it is interrupted by a new station mid-manoeuvre
  • Every manoeuvre returns a zero attitude offset under reduced motion (C1)
  • (atmosphere.test.tsx) Nothing in the manoeuvre path touches the skeleton — the existing no-Neck, no-quaternion.multiply assertions still hold (C7)

Verification: scrolling the length of the page produces at least one loop and at least one roll, they never overlap the text, and interrupting one mid-flight by scrolling fast leaves the dragon in a sane attitude rather than stuck rotated.

U5. Clip vocabulary, phase-matched blending, effort-driven wingbeat

Goal: six clips, chosen with hysteresis, blended in phase, and beating at a rate that reflects what the dragon is doing.

Requirements: R1, R2.

Dependencies: U1. Independent of U2–U4 and can be built alongside them.

Files:

  • modify tools/creatures.json — add glide and takeoff
  • regenerate src/generated/creatures.ts and public/creatures/*.glb via npm run creatures (needs the Unity checkout)
  • create src/webgl/clips.ts — the state machine, hysteresis thresholds, per-pair cross-fade durations, phase matching, effort curve
  • create src/webgl/clips.test.ts
  • modify src/webgl/Creatures.tsx
  • modify src/webgl/atmosphere.test.tsx

Approach: clip choice becomes a pure function of (speed, verticalVelocity, settling, landed, current) returning the next clip name. Hysteresis is expressed as separate enter and leave thresholds, so the state machine cannot oscillate at a boundary — the current single speed > FLYING test is a live chatter risk.

Phase matching sets the incoming action's time to the same proportional point in its own cycle that the outgoing action occupies, before the cross-fade begins. All three of fly, hover and glide are wing cycles of different durations; without this the wings jump mid-beat on every transition.

mixer.timeScale is driven by an effort term — speed plus climb rate, normalised, smoothed, and clamped. Hard climb beats faster; a glide beats slower; the landed idle continues to ease off as it does today.

The duplicate-final-keyframe trim already in Creatures.tsx must apply to the two new clips too — it is the reason the cycles do not hitch.

Patterns to follow: the existing keyframe trim and crossFadeTo call; the generated-file discipline in HANDOFF.md (never hand-edit src/generated/).

Test scenarios (src/webgl/clips.test.ts unless noted):

  • A speed sequence that crosses the threshold, dithers around it, and crosses back produces exactly two clip changes, not a stream of them — the hysteresis claim
  • Descending at speed selects glide; climbing at the same speed selects fly
  • Settling selects landing regardless of speed, and landed selects ground
  • Scrolling back up from landed selects takeoff, and takeoff gives way to fly rather than looping
  • Phase matching maps a cycle position proportionally: an outgoing action 40% through its cycle yields an incoming time 40% through a cycle of different length
  • Every ordered clip pair has a defined cross-fade duration, and none is zero
  • The effort curve is monotonic in speed, bounded at both ends, and returns the resting value at zero speed
  • (atmosphere.test.tsx) The generated manifest carries all six clips, and the total payload is asserted against a stated ceiling so a future clip addition is a deliberate act rather than a silent regression

Verification: transitions between hover, flight and glide show no wing jump. Wings visibly beat harder on a climb than on a glide. Nothing flickers when the dragon idles near the travel threshold.

U6. Take-off, and splitting the load so the dragon appears sooner

Goal: scrolling up from the landing reads as a launch, and the two added clips do not delay the dragon's first appearance.

Requirements: R2, R6. Preserves C2, C4.

Dependencies: U5.

Files:

  • modify src/webgl/Creatures.tsx — split the loader, add the launch impulse
  • modify src/webgl/atmosphere.test.tsx

Approach: useLoader suspends, so today the dragon waits on all four clips before it appears; six would be worse. Split into a flight set (mesh, hover, fly, glide) loaded with the component, and an arrival set (landing, ground, takeoff) in a child component behind its own Suspense boundary that mounts once progress passes the halfway mark and registers its actions into the parent mixer through a callback. That gives the arrival set half a page of scrolling to arrive, and the flight set — all the visitor sees for the first half — loads roughly a third lighter than today.

The clip state machine must tolerate an action that is not yet present and fall back to the nearest available clip rather than throwing or freezing. This is the main risk in the unit and the tests below target it directly.

Launch: on release from the landing, play takeoff once and add an outward and upward impulse to the local velocity, so it springs off the bar instead of being drawn away by the spring.

Execution note: build the fallback path test-first. It is the failure mode that only appears on a slow connection, which is exactly the case no manual pass will reproduce.

Test scenarios (src/webgl/atmosphere.test.tsx):

  • The flight set and the arrival set are separate load boundaries, and the arrival set is not requested at progress zero
  • Requesting a clip that has not loaded returns the nearest available clip and does not throw
  • Reaching the bottom before the arrival set has loaded holds hover and adopts landing when it arrives, rather than freezing or snapping
  • The launch impulse is applied exactly once per release, is directed away from the footer, and is not applied when the dragon was never landed
  • Reduced motion skips the impulse (C1)
  • The dragon still lands only at the very bottom, on its feet, on the measured footer line — the existing settle assertions continue to pass unchanged (C4)

Verification: on a throttled connection the dragon appears and flies during the first half of the page, and the landing is ready by the time the reader reaches it. Scrolling up from the landing shows a push off the bar.

Tests That Change

The existing suite carries roughly sixty dragon assertions, most of them regular expressions matched over src/webgl/Creatures.tsx. HANDOFF.md already records that this style breaks on refactors that preserve behaviour, and this plan is a large refactor that preserves behaviour. Named explicitly so the rewrite is a deliberate act rather than a scramble:

Must survive, restated against the new data: clearance from the reading column on all three axes; alternating sides; nose-up, banked, three-quarter resting attitude; distinct attitudes between resting places; landing only at the very bottom; squared up to the reader on arrival; overdamped approach; feet on the measured footer line; warm-range thresholds; flat shading; no skeleton access; no pointer tracking; no fire; hidden until measured; reduced motion holds it still.

Will be replaced: perchFor and the __PERCHES-per-layer assertions become stationAt and the route table; rest: 'hover' as a per-perch string becomes the clip state machine; the addScaledVector(s.velocity, delta) source match becomes a behavioural framerate-independence test; the Math.min(rawDelta, 1 / 30) match follows the new clamp; the 0.75 - s.settled * 0.25 match follows the effort curve.

Should be added: wherever a source-regex test is replaced, prefer an assertion against an exported constant or a pure function over a new regex. That is the whole reason U1 extracts src/webgl/flight.ts.

Risks

The reading column is the constraint most at risk. Curved routes, drift and manoeuvres all add swept volume that straight-line transits did not have. Every one of the three is gated by the same clearance rule, tested independently, and the loop's bulge is directed outward by construction rather than by tuning.

Interrupting a manoeuvre. A reader who scrolls fast can change station mid-loop. The additive channel must unwind rather than snap, and the dive must restore the physics constants on any exit path, not only the normal one. Called out in U4's scenarios.

Payload. 650KB to roughly 850KB. Mitigated by the split in U6 and bounded by a ceiling assertion in U5, but it is a real increase and it lands on the top tier only.

Per-frame cost. Curve evaluation, manoeuvre state and a fixed-timestep accumulator all add work. The zero-allocation discipline is the thing to hold — new scratch vectors go in state.current like the existing ones. The substep count is bounded by the delta clamp.

Regenerating the clips needs the Unity checkout. npm run creatures reads ~/Documents/Unity Project. The committed public/creatures/ output means a clean clone still builds, but U5 cannot be completed without the checkout present.

Deferred to Follow-Up Work

  • Wyvern@Falling and Wyvern@GlideToLanding remain unused. A glide-in landing may read better than the current stationary-to-landing once routes are curved; worth revisiting after U3 lands, not before.
  • No real device has run any of this. Frame rate on mid-range mobile is still unmeasured — see the known limits in docs/verification.md. This plan makes the motion framerate-independent, which reduces but does not remove the need.
  • The frameloop="demand" claim in src/webgl/Atmosphere.tsx is no longer true while the dragon is mounted, since its frame body invalidates unconditionally. Harmless today; worth an honest comment or a genuine idle gate later.

Outside This Change

Page copy, layout, room tones, the colour booklet, the roadmap field, the scroll system, and the tier gate. The dragon does not track the pointer and does not breathe fire — both were built and removed on request, and nothing here reopens either.
