Verified in a browser

Checked at a 320×720 viewport, which is narrower than any phone this needs to serve, and at desktop.

Check Result
Horizontal overflow at 320px None. Document scroll width equals client width.
Elements extending past the viewport None.
Tap targets below the 44px floor None, after the two fixes below.
WebGL canvas mounts and sits behind every layer Yes — z-index: 0 against layers at 1.
Canvas hidden from assistive technology Yes — aria-hidden, pointer-events: none.
Room tone changes while object colour holds Yes, confirmed by eye across all four tones.
Typefaces load and render at their specified weights Yes — Grenze, Familjen Grotesk, Martian Mono.

Two tap-target defects were found and fixed during this pass:

  • Area groups in the field measured about 25px tall. They now carry the system's --size-tap-min floor.
  • The token is 40px by the design system's own sizing, which is under the floor. Its hit area was grown to 44px while the disc stays at 40 — redrawing the token at the wrong size to satisfy a tap rule would have been the wrong trade.

Verified in a browser — the dragon's flight

Checked at 430×900 (a phone in portrait) and at 1410×1203, on a fresh load each time, with the live values read out of the frame loop rather than judged by eye.

Check Result
Visible and fully in frame at the opening, portrait Yes — after the placement rework. It was entirely off the side before.
Visible and fully in frame at the opening, desktop Yes. It was clipped by the left edge before.
Clear of the text through the roadmap, portrait Body in the gutter; a wingtip grazes the occasional small label. See the residual below.
Clear of the text through the roadmap, desktop Yes.
Only the flight set is fetched before it appears Yes — wyvern.glb, fly, hover, glide. No arrival clips.
Arrival clips fetched by the time the bottom is reached Yes — all six present, from the second boundary.
Lands at the very bottom Yes — settled reaches 1, landed true, playing the grounded idle.
Feet on the footer bar rather than a wingtip Yes — ten foot and toe bones found; the lowest converges onto the measured bar line to within 1e-7.
Lands at full size, not the narrow-window size Yes.

Four defects were found during this pass and fixed. Each is recorded as a gotcha in HANDOFF.md, because each looked like something else first:

  • Stations stated in world units put the creature off the side of a phone.
  • The landing was being routed, so it never arrived.
  • The landing was being shrunk by the narrow-window rule that exists to keep it off text there is none of beside the footer.
  • An unsized camera reports an aspect of zero over zero, and one NaN frame poisons the eased size for the rest of the session.

Residual, stated rather than implied. On a 430-wide viewport the reading column is 88% of the screen. There is no placement where a visible creature never touches it. It now sits in the gutter at about a sixth of the screen width and grazes the occasional label, against crossing headlines at half the width before. On a desktop window the column is 44% and there is real margin.

Verified in a browser — the published site

Checked against https://austin-blair.github.io/isekai-showcase/, not a local server, because the failure this guards against only appears once the site is served from a subdirectory rather than a domain root.

Check Result
Every route resolves /, the three document pages, robots.txt, the fonts, the creature geometry and the Draco decoder all 200.
Requests that failed None.
Typefaces under the subpath All three load. This is the case that breaks first: Vite leaves a url(/fonts/…) into the public directory alone, so without the rewrite the page silently falls back to system fonts.
WebGL canvas initialises Yes — drawing buffer sized to the viewport rather than the 300×150 default.
Creature geometry and decoder Both load from /isekai-showcase/, and the dragon flies.
Colour booklet 598 swatches present.
Kept out of search noindex, nofollow on every page, and robots.txt disallows everything.

One diagnosis worth keeping. Mid-check the dragon stopped loading and the base path looked responsible. It was not: stashing the change and finding the same failure at the previous commit showed the browser had run out of WebGL contexts after a long session of reloads. The tell is a canvas left at its default 300×150 with correctly sized parents — the renderer never initialised. A fresh tab clears it. Verify a graphics change in a new tab before believing a regression.

Verified without a browser

  • 373 tests across the generators, the copy, the page, the objects, the scroll layer, the field, the 3D gate, and the flight — the spring, the routes, the manoeuvres, the clip machine and the placement each have their own suite of pure functions with no WebGL context.
  • No JavaScript at all: the built dist/index.html contains all five layers as real HTML, including the opening lines, the loop, the objects section, the unbuilt count, the doors and the contact address.
  • No border declaration anywhere in the shipped CSS. No <hr>. No gold heading. Nothing scaling past 1.04. Nothing fading in.
  • No internal vocabulary in any visitor-facing string, and no internal identifier in the rendered page.

Weight

What a device on the lowest tier downloads:

File Raw gzip brotli
index.html (prerendered) 278.8 KB 19.7 KB 13.1 KB
index.js 265.2 KB 79.5 KB 67.5 KB
index.css 24.3 KB 5.6 KB 4.8 KB
Total 568.3 KB 104.8 KB 85.4 KB

The HTML is large raw and small compressed, because 598 swatches of prerendered markup are as repetitive as markup gets — it compresses fourteen to one. The raw figure is the one worth ignoring; the wire figure is the one that matters.

Plus 224 KB of self-hosted fonts, cached after first visit.

The three.js chunk is 848 KB and is not referenced from the HTML at all. It is fetched only when the device tier is above zero, so a phone that will not use it never pays for it. The dragon is a further 62 KB of code and 914 KB of geometry and clips, on the top tier only, split across two boundaries:

Set Files Size When
Flight mesh, hover, fly, glide 490 KB with the component
Arrival takeoff, landing, ground 424 KB once the reader passes halfway

First appearance therefore costs 490 KB — less than the 650 KB it cost before glide and takeoff were added at all, because the three clips that only matter at the very bottom no longer hold it up.

Not verified

Stated plainly rather than implied:

  • No real device has run this. Every check above was made in a desktop browser at a phone-sized viewport. Viewport size is not thermal behaviour, and it is not iOS Safari.
  • Frame rate on mid-range hardware is unmeasured. The draw-call budget is argued structurally — one instanced mesh for every mote plus three shaft cones, four calls against a mobile budget of about fifty — but no frame time has been recorded on real hardware.
  • Context loss has not been observed in the wild. The teardown path is unit-tested against a synthetic event. It has not been triggered by actually backgrounding the page on an iPhone, which is the case it exists for.
  • The device tier heuristic is untuned. It combines a WebGL probe, core count, pointer type and viewport size, and biases downward when they disagree. Whether it makes the right call on real mid-range Android is unknown.

The pass that still needs doing

  2. Background and foreground the tab several times, then keep scrolling. The page must either recover or fall back to the DOM page. It must never show a black screen.
  3. Watch for thermal throttling on a long scroll.
  4. Record what happened in this file.
